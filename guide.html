1 What Is This Tool?

The big picture — what it does and why it matters

The Healthcare Travel Staffing Term Sheet is an online calculator that helps staffing companies figure out the financial details of a travel healthcare assignment before they commit to it.

Think of It Like This

Imagine you're a staffing company. A hospital in Rochester, NY needs a travel nurse for 13 weeks. You need to answer:

  • What do I pay the nurse? (hourly rate + housing stipend + meals)
  • What do I charge the hospital? (the bill rate)
  • What are my costs? (background checks, drug screens, workers comp, taxes)
  • Am I making money on this deal? (profit margins)
  • How long until my upfront costs are covered? (weeks to break even)

This tool answers all of those questions in real time as you type. Change one number and everything recalculates instantly.

Key Benefit You can model different scenarios in seconds. "What if I pay $22/hr instead of $20?" — just change the number and immediately see how it affects your margin, markup, and profitability.

Who Is This For?

RoleHow They Use It
Account ManagersPrice new assignments and negotiate bill rates with hospitals
RecruitersDetermine competitive pay packages for travel nurses
Operations / FinanceVerify margins and ensure deals are profitable before approval
Business OwnersGet a snapshot of deal health across their portfolio

What Happens to My Data?

  • Your data is saved only in your browser (on your device). Nothing is sent to a server.
  • If you close the browser and come back, your last session is waiting for you.
  • Use Print/PDF to save a permanent copy.

VMS Fee (Vendor Management System)

Many hospitals use a VMS (like ShiftWise, Medefis, or SAP Fieldglass) that takes a percentage of your bill rate as a fee. This reduces your actual revenue.

1
Enter the VMS Fee % (e.g., 4.00 for a 4% fee)
2
A new "Net Bill Rate" row appears showing your bill rate after the VMS cut
Net Bill Rate = Bill Rate × (1 - VMS%)
Net Bill Rate = $75.00 × (1 - 0.04) = $72.00/hr
Important All margin and profit calculations use the net bill rate (after VMS), not the gross bill rate. A 4% VMS fee on a $75/hr bill rate costs you $3.00/hr — that's $108/week or $1,404 over a 13-week contract.

How Expense Amortization Works

You spend $570 once, but you need to know how it affects your per-hour profit. The tool spreads this cost across all contract hours:

Expense Burden Per Hour = Total Expenses ÷ Total Contract Hours
Expense Burden Per Hour = $570.00 ÷ (36 hrs × 13 wks)
Expense Burden Per Hour = $570.00 ÷ 468 = $1.22/hr

This $1.22/hr gets added to your cost side in every margin calculation.

GSA Stipends Are Excluded Burdens only apply to taxable wages ($20.00/hr in our example). The $34.03/hr in GSA stipends is tax-free, so you don't pay WC or ER on it. This is a significant cost savings on travel assignments.

The tool includes a built-in minimum wage protection system that prevents you from entering a pay rate below the applicable state or city minimum wage.

How It Works 1. When you enter a facility zip code, the tool identifies the state and city.
2. If you enter a regular pay rate below the minimum wage, a warning banner appears.
3. The pay rate is automatically corrected to the applicable minimum wage.
4. The Profitable Term Sheet Calculator also uses this as a floor — it will never suggest a rate below minimum wage.

8 Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions

Q: Do I need to fill in every field?

No. The financial engine needs these minimum fields to work: Hours/Week, Contract Weeks, Pay Rate, and Bill Rate. Everything else adds accuracy but isn't required.

Q: What's the difference between Gross Margin and Net Margin?

Gross Margin = Bill - Pay. It's the simple spread before any costs.
Net Margin = Net Bill - Total Cost (pay + taxes + WC + expenses). It's your actual profit.

Q: Why does my Net Markup show 0.00x?

This usually means you haven't entered a bill rate yet, or the bill rate is $0. Enter a bill rate and it will calculate instantly.

Q: What happens if I don't enter Contract Weeks?

The tool switches to a "weekly snapshot" mode. Expenses are estimated per-week instead of amortized over the full contract. For the most accurate expense burden, always enter weeks.

Q: Can I use this on my phone?

Yes! The layout automatically adapts for mobile. The form is full-width at top, with three columns below showing Candidate Compensation, Margins, and Profit Breakdowns side by side.

Q: Is my data secure?

Your data stays in your browser only (localStorage). It's never sent to any server. If you clear your browser data, it's gone. Use Print/PDF to keep a permanent copy.

Q: How do I compare two scenarios (e.g., different pay rates)?

Open the tool in two browser tabs. Set up the same base deal in both, then change the variable you want to compare (e.g., $20/hr vs $22/hr). View them side-by-side.

Q: Why are GSA stipends separate from pay?

GSA stipends (housing and meals) are tax-free for the traveler and burden-free for the company. You don't pay Workers Comp or employer taxes on stipends, which saves significant money. The tool keeps them separate to calculate burdens correctly.

Q: What does "Burden % of GM" tell me?

It tells you what percentage of your gross margin goes to overhead costs (expenses + employer taxes + workers comp). If it's over 50%, more than half your spread is going to costs — not profit.

Q: How do I save a PDF?

Click the "Print/PDF" button at the bottom of the form. In the print dialog, select "Save as PDF" as the destination. The sidebars are hidden in print mode so you get a clean document.

Q: How does the Bill Rate Multiplier work?

When you enter a regular bill rate, an OT Multiplier row appears. Enter a number (e.g., 1.5) and the OT bill rate auto-fills as Regular × Multiplier. It updates in real-time whenever the regular bill rate changes. The multiplier also works for DT rates in states like California.

Q: What does "Break-Even" mean in the OT analysis?

Break-even is the point where your OT revenue exactly covers your OT costs (pay + burden). The "Max OT Pay" tells you the highest pay rate where you don't lose money. If your current OT pay is above that number, every OT hour costs you money.

Q: How does the Profitable Term Sheet Calculator work?

Enter a Target Net Margin % and the tool reverse-calculates the maximum regular pay rate that achieves that margin, factoring in your bill rate, burden, VMS fee, stipends, and expenses. It also respects minimum wage laws — it will never suggest a rate below the applicable state or city minimum.

Q: Will the tool stop me from entering below minimum wage?

Yes. If you enter a regular pay rate below the applicable minimum wage (based on the facility zip code), the tool shows a warning and auto-corrects the rate to the minimum. This works with all 50 states plus major city overrides like Seattle, San Francisco, and Denver.

9 Glossary of Terms

Industry terms defined in plain language

TermDefinition
Bill RateThe hourly rate you charge the hospital. This is your revenue.
Pay RateThe hourly rate you pay the traveler. This is your largest cost.
Blended RateThe combined hourly value of pay + stipends. What the traveler "earns" per hour in total value.
BurdenCosts you pay on top of wages — employer taxes, workers comp, insurance, and amortized expenses.
Burden RateThe dollar amount per hour that burden costs you (e.g., $4.80/hr).
GSA Per DiemGovernment-set maximum daily rates for housing and meals in a specific city. Used as the basis for tax-free travel stipends.
Gross MarginBill minus Pay — the raw spread before any costs are deducted.
Net MarginWhat's left after ALL costs (VMS, pay, taxes, WC, expenses). Your true profit.
MarkupA multiplier showing how much more you bill than your costs. 1.20x = you bill 20% more than your costs.
VMSVendor Management System — a technology platform hospitals use to manage staffing vendors. They charge a % fee.
WC (Workers Comp)Insurance covering workplace injuries. You pay a premium as a % of taxable wages.
ER CostEmployer-side payroll taxes — Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), federal and state unemployment taxes, and any benefits.
AmortizeTo spread a one-time cost across multiple hours. Example: $570 in expenses spread over 468 hours = $1.22/hr.
OT (Overtime)Hours worked beyond the threshold (usually 40/week or 8/day depending on state). Paid at 1.5x the regular rate.
DT (Double Time)Hours worked beyond an extended threshold (e.g., 12 hrs/day in California). Paid at 2x the regular rate.
M&IMeals & Incidental Expenses — part of the GSA per diem rate covering food and small expenses.
StipendA fixed allowance paid to the traveler for housing or meals. Tax-free when based on GSA rates.
Contract HoursTotal hours in the assignment = Hours/Week × Weeks. Used to amortize flat expenses.
Bill Rate MultiplierA factor applied to the regular bill rate to auto-calculate OT/DT bill rates (e.g., 1.5x for OT, 2.0x for DT).
Break-Even RateThe pay or bill rate at which profit is exactly $0 — no gain, no loss. Used to evaluate OT profitability.
Target NM%Your desired Net Margin percentage. Used by the Profitable Term Sheet Calculator to reverse-engineer suggested pay rates.
Minimum WageThe legally mandated lowest hourly pay rate, set by federal, state, or city law. The tool enforces the highest applicable rate.
OT Gross MarginThe raw spread on overtime hours: (Net OT Bill - OT Pay) × OT Hours per week.
